How to use (short version)

  1. Press Normal day first. Note u = 4.5% and π = 2%.
  2. Press Hot economy: unemployment falls, inflation rises — the dot slides up the short-run curve.
  3. Press Expectations catch up: the whole curve shifts up and inflation rises again, even though unemployment is unchanged.
  4. Press Central bank raises rates: demand cools, unemployment walks back to the natural rate — but inflation stays high. That is the long run.
  5. Press Stagflation: unemployment is high AND inflation is high. The curve itself moved, not just the dot.
  6. Move the sliders by hand and watch the dot and the piston follow.
Honest limits — read before believing anything:
  • This is a teaching model, not a forecast. The expectations-augmented Phillips curve is a textbook simplification; real inflation also moves with productivity, global prices, and policy lags this lab leaves out.
  • The gas numbers are a translation of the Phillips numbers, not a measurement of a real market. Z here is computed from the mapping (Z = ΠV/(QT)); it is a way to see the model, not a law of nature. The operational definition of T is still being pinned down in the UTPF work.
  • Nothing here is investment advice. Do not buy or sell anything from these numbers.
