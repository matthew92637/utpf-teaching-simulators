# What is the AI Gas Bottle simulator good for?

The value of this simulator is that it turns something very big and very scary into a bottle you can see and touch.

**First, big things become small.** AI, unemployment, banks, bubbles — these are big words, and they sound frightening. But the bottle is small: money comes in, real things come out, expectations run hot. When you can see how tight the bottle is, you stop being afraid.

**Second, you see how one thing pushes over the next.** One person losing a job looks like a small thing. But when many people lose their jobs together, bad debts grow, and the bank cannot hold. The simulator shows you this chain, like dominoes falling one after another.

**Third, you can try first without real danger.** The real world cannot be rewound. The simulator can: press one button and the company goes bankrupt; press another and it survives. It is like practising building a castle in the sand first, so the real one does not fall down.

**Fourth, you only need to watch one number.** The news gives you a hundred numbers every day — too many to follow. The simulator teaches you to watch just one: Z. Near 1, the bottle is healthy; below 0.5, danger. It is like a thermometer: a fever tells you to rest.

## How to use it in daily life

The money at home is a bottle too. What mum and dad earn every month is V; what the credit card spends first is Q. If you spend a lot on the card before next month's pay arrives, the bottle gets very tight. What the simulator teaches is simple: do not sign a contract bigger than next month's pay.

A small shop owner stocking goods is the same. Order a lot of stock first, customers have not come yet, the cash runs out, and the shop closes. The clever way is: order a little, sell it, then order more. That is exactly what the "prudent" version does.

Sharing money in the family is the 3% tax idea. One person earns a lot, another loses their job, and the one who earns shares a little to help — so the family bottle does not run empty.

It helps when you watch the news too. Do not be frightened when you see "AI will take all the jobs." Just ask three questions: how much money is coming in? How much real stuff is being made? How tight is the bottle? If the news cannot answer, it is only a story, not something actually measured.

It is best for teaching students. In five minutes of pressing buttons, students see with their own eyes how the bottle breaks — far better than memorising a whole lesson from a book. That is why teaching simulators exist: so students can feel the market with their own hands, instead of memorising it.

And remember: the simulator cannot tell you what the future will be. What it can do is let you practise once in the sand before you make a real decision.
