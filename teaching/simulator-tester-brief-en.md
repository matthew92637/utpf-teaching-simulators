# Test a market-as-gas simulator — 30-minute tester brief

**From:** Prof. Matthew Yen, Fresno State (emeritus), Industrial Technology
**Contact:** matthewy@mail.fresnostate.edu

## What it is

Two teaching simulators that model a market the way physics models a gas:
price behaves like pressure, money behaves like gas in a container, and a
"piston" settles at the market-clearing price. You run them in a browser.
No installation, no account, no real money involved.

1. **Farmers-market simulator** — a teaching market where you change demand
   and supply and watch the piston price settle.
2. **Short-term forecast simulator** — an ensemble forecast shown as an 80%
   cone around a fair-value anchor, so you can see what the model expects
   and where it is unsure.

## What it is NOT

- Not investment advice. Not a stock forecast. Not for real-money decisions.
- Everything you see is **model-market behaviour**, not a prediction about
  any real stock.

## What you do (about 30 minutes)

1. Open the simulator file in your browser (files are shared with you
   directly — please do not re-post or redistribute them).
2. In the farmers-market simulator, change demand and supply and watch the
   piston price. Does it settle where you expected? Does it converge after
   a shock, or wander?
3. In the forecast simulator, look at the 80% cone against the past week.
   Does the real path stay inside the cone? Where does the cone fail?
4. Answer the five questions below (5 minutes). Optional: stay 15 more
   minutes for a group discussion.

## Five feedback questions

1. Could you run the simulators without help? If not, where did you get stuck?
2. What was confusing — a word, a button, a graph, an idea?
3. Did the piston price settle where you expected after a demand or supply shock?
4. What is the one thing you would change?
5. Would you use this to explain how a market works to a friend? Why or why not?
